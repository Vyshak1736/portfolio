The problem

Operations teams need customer requirements, helper records, assignments, and payment status to stay connected across the service lifecycle. CHAP supports that workflow in a shared internal system.

What it does

  • Customer and helper records connected to job-assignment workflows.
  • Role-based APIs for internal operational access.
  • Payment integration and asynchronous notification workflows.
  • Operational reporting, database-query optimization, and production release work.

How it works

Capture requirements

Staff record customer requirements and maintain relevant helper details.

Coordinate assignments

Operational workflows connect customer requirements to helper and job records.

Track payments and updates

Backend integrations track payment status and queue relevant updates.

Review operations

Reports help staff follow service progress and operational outcomes.

Design decisions

  • My work covers backend architecture, role-based APIs, asynchronous integrations, query optimization, and production releases.
  • Use background processing for integrations and notifications alongside a relational application database.
  • Describe responsibilities and system boundaries publicly while keeping employer source code and operational records private.

Current scope

Employer-owned system. No public repository, credentials, customer data, or internal screenshots are published here. The architecture explorer elsewhere in this portfolio is a reference design, not a complete claim about CHAP deployment.
